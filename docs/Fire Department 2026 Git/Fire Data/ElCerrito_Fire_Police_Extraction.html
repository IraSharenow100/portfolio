Bottom line on your call to flag metric-definition instability: confirmed. Response-time and clearance-rate figures for Police appear exclusively in prose, change their framing/baseline year to year, and are never tied to the standardized KPI table format the department uses for calls-for-service/citations/crime counts.

5. Department goals / stated priorities (condensed)

Fire, FY24-25/25-26 (BB25 pp.161-162): all-risk emergency response, disaster preparedness (CERT program restart, EOC/EOP maintenance), fire prevention/code enforcement and vegetation management, EMS/training standards, facility & apparatus readiness, grant writing, wellness/fitness program, public education, Kensington contract administration. FY23-24 accomplishments highlighted (BB25 p.160): auto-aid agreements with Albany/Berkeley, hired a civilian fire prevention officer, new SOGs for commercial-building/EV/mass-evacuation incidents, grant submissions for a Type 1 engine ($1.4M) and SCBA ($400K), joined Eastbay Hills Wildfire Prevention MOU, wellness program launch, hired 3 firefighters, “saved $17,801,300 in property value.”

Police, FY24-25/25-26 (BB25 p.174-175): core services (emergency response, investigation, traffic safety), community engagement/education, crime-reduction strategy, workplace/retention investment. FY23-24 narrative (BB25 p.174): reached full staffing briefly in Dec. 2023 then fell to 4 vacancies by May 2024; deployed 40 license-plate-reader cameras (July 2023); notable case work (a shooting, an attempted murder, a multi-jurisdictional homicide investigation); deployed a Community Liaison Officer; planned annual town halls. FY24-25 focus: expand body-worn/in-car camera tech, drones/UAS, “drone first responder” strategy exploration, modernize parking enforcement near transit, deploy a facility dog, restore a traffic enforcement officer once staffing allows (BB25 p.175).
