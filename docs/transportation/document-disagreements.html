Where City Documents Disagree

Last updated September 20, 2026.

This page lists 17 places where City, state or agency documents give different facts, dates or numbers for the same thing. Each item names the two documents, their dates and the page, slide or section, so that each can be checked. The last line of each item explains the difference. A difference is not a finding of error; it shows where a document may need to be corrected or explained.

The state agreements, the May 18, 2026 letter, the May 19-20, 2026 emails and the Round 9 application are not posted online. Copies can be obtained from the City or the state as public records. Links to every posted document are at the bottom of the page.

1. Inclusionary housing threshold (the size of project that must include affordable homes)

One document says: 9 or more rental units and 10 or more for-sale units

Where: City of El Cerrito, San Pablo Avenue Specific Plan web page (printed copy), pages 4 and 5

Another document says: Rental projects: minimum size 10 units. For-sale projects: minimum size 9 units

Where: Strategic Economics, Affordable Housing and Anti-Displacement Strategy, July 7, 2026, slide 13

The difference: The two documents reverse the numbers for rental and for-sale projects.

2. Number of Specific Plan goals

One document says: “There are six goals identified in the Plan,” followed by a list of five goals (Goals A to E)

Where: City of El Cerrito, San Pablo Avenue Specific Plan web page (printed copy), page 5

Another document says: The same page then lists five goals, Goals A to E

Where: City of El Cerrito, San Pablo Avenue Specific Plan web page (printed copy), page 5

The difference: The page says six goals but lists only five.

3. Number of Priority Development Areas (PDAs)

One document says: The Specific Plan “identifies three Priority Development Areas”

Where: City of El Cerrito, San Pablo Avenue Specific Plan web page (printed copy), page 4

Another document says: City Council and the Association of Bay Area Governments designated San Pablo Avenue as one Mixed-use Corridor PDA in 2007

Where: San Pablo Avenue Specific Plan Update, Chapter 1: Introduction, October 13, 2022 version, PDF page 9

The difference: The web page says three PDAs; the plan chapter describes one.

4. Which Housing Element the Specific Plan supports

One document says: The Specific Plan was adopted in 2014 “to advance the goals of the City’s 2023-2031 Housing Element”

Where: City of El Cerrito, San Pablo Avenue Specific Plan web page (printed copy), page 4

Another document says: The 2023-2031 Housing Element was adopted August 15, 2023. The Housing Element in place in 2014 and 2015 was certified in May 2015

Where: City Council Agenda Bill, Item 6.D, General Plan Annual Progress Report, March 17, 2026, page 2; San Pablo Avenue Specific Plan Update, Chapter 1: Introduction, October 13, 2022 version, PDF page 4

The difference: A plan adopted in 2014 cannot have been adopted to advance a Housing Element adopted in 2023.

5. Homes built

One document says: 464 homes finished on 7 sites, 2019 to 2025

Where: City of El Cerrito, Economic Development web page, statement on housing and commercial space completed

Another document says: “Delivered 400 market rate, affordable and mixed-use housing units” since the Specific Plan was adopted in 2014

Where: Strategic Economics, Implementation Strategy for the San Pablo Avenue Specific Plan, July 7, 2026, slide 10

The difference: The second figure covers a longer period (2014 to 2026) that includes the first (2019 to 2025), yet it is smaller.

6. Hana Gardens (senior housing)

One document says: 63 units

Where: Strategic Economics, Affordable Housing and Anti-Displacement Strategy, July 7, 2026, slide 11

Another document says: 62 units, “successfully completed in 2018” (May 6, 2025 bill); 62 units, year built 2019 (Housing Successor report)

Where: City Council Agenda Bill, Item 9.C, May 6, 2025 (AHSC application, Parcel C East), page 2; City of El Cerrito, Housing Successor Annual Report for Fiscal Year 2024/2025, Attachment 3 to Agenda Item 6.D, March 17, 2026, page 8

The difference: The documents give different unit counts (63 and 62) and different completion years (2018 and 2019).

7. Street number of the second Plaza building (Parcel C East)

One document says: 6617 Fairmount Avenue

Where: City Council Agenda Bill, Item 9.C, May 6, 2025 (AHSC application, Parcel C East), page 1

Another document says: 6671 Fairmount Avenue

Where: Draft resolution attached to Agenda Bill 9.C, May 6, 2025 (AHSC application, Parcel C East), page 1; City Council Agenda Bill, Item 9.B, May 6, 2025 (predevelopment loan, Parcel C East), page 1; City of El Cerrito, Housing Successor Annual Report for Fiscal Year 2024/2025, Attachment 3 to Agenda Item 6.D, March 17, 2026, page 7

The difference: The same building has two different street numbers.

8. Year of the Climate Action and Adaptation Plan

One document says: “the 2024 Climate Action and Adaptation Plan”

Where: City of El Cerrito, Richmond Street Complete Streets project FAQ, printed September 20, 2026, page 10

Another document says: “2023 Climate Action and Adaptation Plan”

Where: City of El Cerrito, Richmond Street Complete Streets project FAQ, printed September 20, 2026, page 17

The difference: The same FAQ gives two different years for the same plan.

9. Total cost of Richmond Street

One document says: “$11.092 million in funding” (the City’s Richmond Street FAQ, printed September 20, 2026)

Where: City of El Cerrito, Richmond Street Complete Streets project FAQ, printed September 20, 2026, page 3

Another document says: $12,878,610 for all phases, including City administration ($12,798,340 for CCTA activities), adopted March 3, 2026

Where: City Council Agenda Bill, Item 6.H, Amendment to Cooperative Agreement with the Contra Costa Transportation Authority for the Richmond Street Complete Streets Improvement Project, March 3, 2026, pages 4 and 5

The difference: The City’s web page is about $1.8 million lower than the amended plan. It has not been updated.

10. Date of the Richmond Street cooperative agreement

One document says: Cooperative Agreement No. 665 was entered into “on September 19, 2023”

Where: Resolution attached to Agenda Bill 6.H, March 3, 2026, page 2

Another document says: Cooperative Agreement No. 665 was entered into “on October 19, 2023”

Where: Exhibit A (Amendment No. 1 to Cooperative Agreement No. 665), attached to Agenda Bill 6.H, March 3, 2026, page 1

The difference: Two documents in the same agenda item give dates one month apart for the same agreement.

11. BART grant for del Norte

One document says: $812,977

Where: BART Board memo, Safe Routes to BART Cycle 2 awards, July 31, 2023, revised chart

Another document says: $1.5 million

Where: BART Safe Routes to BART Cycle 2 fact sheet for El Cerrito del Norte, June 5, 2025, El Cerrito del Norte project entry

The difference: BART’s own documents give different amounts for the same grant. The 2023 memo corrects the $1.5 million figure.

12. Date the first Plaza grant agreement was signed

One document says: The Standard Agreement was entered into “on or about October 24, 2025”

Where: AHSC Round 8 Disbursement Agreement, El Cerrito Plaza Parcel A South, dated for reference November 20, 2025, page 1

Another document says: Signed October 31, 2025 (City and Related) and November 5, 2025 (HCD)

Where: State Standard Agreement No. 24-AHSC-18468 (AHSC Round 8, El Cerrito Plaza Parcel A South), signed October 31 and November 5, 2025, pages 1 and 2

The difference: The Disbursement Agreement gives a date earlier than either signature date.

13. Which state program guidelines apply

One document says: Guidelines “dated October 31, 2019, amended December 9, 2019”

Where: AHSC Round 8 Disbursement Agreement, El Cerrito Plaza Parcel A South, dated for reference November 20, 2025, page 1

Another document says: “Round 8 Program Guidelines dated December 14, 2023”; the same agreement’s scoring table refers to “AHSC Round 7 Guidelines”

Where: State Standard Agreement No. 24-AHSC-18468 (AHSC Round 8, El Cerrito Plaza Parcel A South), signed October 31 and November 5, 2025, pages 3 and 34

The difference: The two grant documents cite different guidelines, and the Standard Agreement cites two different rounds.

14. How a change to the state grant’s bikeway list can be approved

One document says: A revision to the transportation work “shall require an amendment”; the Disbursement Agreement may not be amended “except in writing executed by all parties”

Where: State Standard Agreement No. 24-AHSC-18468 (AHSC Round 8, El Cerrito Plaza Parcel A South), signed October 31 and November 5, 2025, page 6, Exhibit A section 3.C; AHSC Round 8 Disbursement Agreement, El Cerrito Plaza Parcel A South, dated for reference November 20, 2025, page 24, section 31

Another document says: “this email alone can serve as approval”

Where: Email chain “AHSC Change Request Form Attachment - 24-AHSC-18468,” May 19-20, 2026 (4 pages), page 1, May 20, 2026, 2:46 PM

The difference: The agreements call for a written amendment; the state’s email says an email is enough. No amendment after the email has been seen.

15. Miles of bikeways in the state grant

One document says: “The Awarded project had a proposed 2.22 miles of bikeways”

Where: Email chain “AHSC Change Request Form Attachment - 24-AHSC-18468,” May 19-20, 2026 (4 pages), page 3, May 20, 2026, 9:28 AM

Another document says: “Current Total (A + C): 2.4 lane miles”

Where: Email chain “AHSC Change Request Form Attachment - 24-AHSC-18468,” May 19-20, 2026 (4 pages), page 2, May 20, 2026, 10:32 AM

The difference: The state and the City give different totals for the same bikeways on the same day.

16. Traffic on Richmond Street

One document says: October 2024 sensor count: “ADT of 6,400” vehicles a day

Where: City of El Cerrito, Richmond Street Complete Streets project FAQ, printed September 20, 2026, page 8

Another document says: “about 8,400 vehicles daily” (same FAQ, later page); “9000 AADT” (state email)

Where: City of El Cerrito, Richmond Street Complete Streets project FAQ, printed September 20, 2026, page 20; Email chain “AHSC Change Request Form Attachment - 24-AHSC-18468,” May 19-20, 2026 (4 pages), page 1, May 20, 2026, 2:46 PM

The difference: Three different traffic counts are used for the same street.

17. Number of BART railcars in the Round 9 application

One document says: “two new BART railcars”

Where: AHSC Round 9 application narrative prompts for El Cerrito Plaza Parcel C East, footer dated March 25, 2025 (excerpt: narrative pages 3, 7, 10, 12, 14, 15), PDF page 1 (narrative page 3)

Another document says: “three new BART railcars”

Where: AHSC Round 9 application narrative prompts for El Cerrito Plaza Parcel C East, footer dated March 25, 2025 (excerpt: narrative pages 3, 7, 10, 12, 14, 15), PDF page 5 (narrative page 14)

The difference: The same application narrative gives two different numbers.
