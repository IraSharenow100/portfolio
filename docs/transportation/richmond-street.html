Richmond Street & Complete Streets

Richmond Street & Complete Streets

Richmond Street bike lanes have been funded, sought, and re-funded through at least four separate, overlapping efforts: a federal Complete Streets grant, and three rounds of a state Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities (AHSC) grant tied to two BART-adjacent housing buildings at El Cerrito Plaza. This page documents each thread in order, with reference material on bike lane classifications and the most recent budget data at the end.

Bike Lane Classifications (reference)

Class Description
Class I A separated, off-street path — fully apart from car traffic. The Ohlone Greenway is Class I.
Class II A painted lane on the road, marked with stripes. No physical barrier from car traffic.
Class III A “bike route” — signage and sometimes sharrow markings only. No dedicated lane; cyclists share the travel lane with cars.
Class IV A protected bikeway — physically separated from car traffic with curbs, posts, or parked cars. The safest on-street option short of a fully separated path.

Thread A — SS4A / CCTA “Richmond Street Complete Streets”

  • Project: ~1.85 miles, Hill Street to Fairmount Avenue (Key Blvd/Hill St/Elm St intersection to Richmond St/Fairmount Ave/Ohlone Greenway intersection)
  • Funding as presented: ~$11M (~$8M Safe Streets for All [SS4A] federal grant + ~$3M local match)
  • Presented to Council: July 15, 2025 (Item 9A, study session only — no formal vote on funding)
  • At that time, the bike lane component specifically was not funded; only sidewalks, curb ramps, and traffic calming on the corridor had funding, through SS4A. A Council motion (Ktsanes) to study parking-preserving alternatives failed for lack of a second.
  • Not part of any AHSC application at the time.

Source: July 15, 2025 Council study session materials, Item 9A.

AHSC Grants for El Cerrito Plaza BART Housing (Parcel A South & Parcel C East)

Related California and the City have applied four times for AHSC grants covering two buildings at El Cerrito Plaza BART. Each application proposed a different bikeway package to earn scoring points.

Round 7 (submitted 2023) — Parcel A South, denied

  • Building: Parcel A South, 515 Richmond St. 6-story, 70 affordable apartments (1–3 bedroom).
  • Bikeway proposed: East-West package — Class IV on Central Ave, Class III on Lincoln Ave (San Pablo to Ashbury), Class III on San Diego St/Belmont Ave.
  • GHG claim: 25,533 MTCO2e reduced. HCD score: 76.
  • Result: not awarded.

Round 8 (submitted 2024) — Parcel A South, awarded — “Thread C”

  • Same building, same bikeway package as Round 7, resubmitted.
  • Grant: $39.1 million, Agreement No. 24-AHSC-18468, awarded August 2024.
  • GHG claim: 38,129 MTCO2e reduced. HCD score: 83.
  • This is the grant amended in May 2026 (below).

May 18, 2026 amendment: Public Works Director Yvetteh Ortiz requested deleting the Lincoln Ave segment (1.1 lane miles) and substituting Richmond St/Elm St (2.0 lane miles, Class II) + Central Ave (0.1 mi).

  • May 20, 2026, 9:28 AM: SGC’s A’shanti Coleman initially rejected the amendment (project scoring dropped from 4 to 1).
  • May 20, 2026, 2:46 PM: SGC’s Marc Caswell reversed that rejection the same day and approved it by email, citing a “lapse in reading comprehension.”

The original package included a Class IV (protected) segment on Central Ave plus two Class III segments and connected to the Ohlone Greenway, a Class I path running the length of El Cerrito. The Richmond St substitute is Class II (paint only, no physical protection) and sits about three blocks east of the Ohlone Greenway, adding limited new network connectivity.

Bikeways are a small share of this grant’s claimed GHG reductions (about 1.1%; a BART traction power substation upgrade drives about 91.7%), and SGC does not require GHG recalculation for bikeway changes below a 10% threshold — meaning the swap had limited effect on grant compliance even though bikeways likely drove a disproportionate share of the original application’s scoring points.

Source: Public Works staff correspondence with SGC, May 18–20, 2026; SGC scoring records; HCD scoring figures for Rounds 7–8 from an internal comparison spreadsheet sourced to sgc.ca.gov — not yet cross-checked against a primary HCD scoring document.

Round 9 (submitted 2025) — Parcel C East, denied — “Thread B”

  • Building: Parcel C East, 84 affordable family units, second phase.
  • Bikeway proposed: Class IV protected bikeway on Richmond Street (Moeser Ln to Hill St, ~2 lane miles) + Class II on Pierce Street.
  • GHG claim: ~33,800 MTCO2e (applicant’s own benefits calculator). Total ask: $42.4 million.
  • Result: denied by SGC, December 10, 2025.

This was the funding specifically sought for Richmond St bike lanes under this application — it failed. The same Richmond St segment was then substituted into the already-awarded Round 8 grant five months later (see above).

Source: SGC award notification, December 10, 2025.

Round 10 (submitted 2026) — Parcel C East, resubmitted, decision pending

  • Same building, Parcel C East.
  • Bikeway proposed: Class III on Norvell St, Albermarle St, Lincoln Ave (2.2 lane miles) + Class IV on Pierce St (0.4 miles) — Richmond Street dropped entirely from this application.
  • GHG claim: 54,241 MTCO2e — self-reported in the application narrative; not yet verified against a separate benefits calculator.
  • NOFA released March 4, 2026; applications due May 4, 2026; state target award date December 2026.

Total ask: $40.2 million, per the AHSC Round 10 application workbook. Separately, the City’s Community Development Department FY2024-25/FY2025-26 accomplishments report describes Round 10 as seeking “approximately $3.5 million in Complete Streets improvements as part of the City’s AHSC Parcel C East application” — this appears to be the transportation/Complete Streets sub-component within the larger $40.2 million total application, not a conflicting total (consistent with how Round 9’s $42.4 million ask likewise broke out into separate housing and transportation components).

Source: AHSC Round 10 application workbook; FY2026-27/FY2027-28 Biennial Budget, Community Development Department accomplishments section.

Design Standard Concern

The Local Road Safety Plan (Kittelson, July 2026) states that physical separation is required on corridors with speeds above 25 mph. Richmond St exceeds this threshold by the City’s own data, but the project as designed uses paint-only Class II lanes, not physically separated Class IV. The LRSP explicitly excludes Richmond St from its own Backbone Bikeway analysis, deferring instead to the Complete Streets project.

CEQA Notice of Exemption (signed March 19, 2026 by Ortiz, for Thread A) already used the naming “Richmond St and Elm St, Key Blvd to Fairmount Ave” — a documented geographic link between Thread A and the Thread C amendment two months later.

Predevelopment Loans to Related California

Two separate $350,000 predevelopment loans from the City to Related California — one per building, not one loan split two ways. Confirmed from the City’s agenda bills and resolutions for each item.

Loan #1 — Parcel A South

  • Meeting date: February 7, 2023. Agenda item 8.A.
  • Resolution No. 2023-08 (confirmed by a later staff report — the May 6, 2025 Parcel C East agenda bill references “the loan provided in 2023 through Resolution No. 2023-08”). A companion resolution authorizing inclusion of a City-owned parcel in the same application, adopted the same meeting, is Resolution 2023-09.
  • Terms: $350,000 from the Low and Moderate Income Housing Asset Fund; 55-year term from Certificate of Occupancy; 3% annual interest; repayment equal to the City’s proportionate share of residual receipts once constructed (deemed repaid if never built); disbursed in two draws — $200,000 in FY2022-23 and $150,000 in FY2023-24. Total predevelopment budget was $2.6 million; the City’s loan funded about 13% of it.

Loan #2 — Parcel C East

  • Meeting date: May 6, 2025. Agenda item 9.B, for 6671 Fairmount Avenue, 84 affordable units.
  • Resolution 2025-28. Ayes: Wysinger, Quinto, Ktsanes, Saltzman. Councilmember Motoyama recused and left the room due to a potential conflict of interest.
  • Terms (staff report states explicitly structured the same as Loan #1): $350,000 from the same Housing Asset Fund; 55-year term from Certificate of Occupancy; 3% annual interest; repayment solely from Parcel C East cash flow once constructed, secured by a leasehold deed of trust once the parcel is subdivided, subordinate to bank and state financing. Total predevelopment budget was $2.7 million; the City’s loan funded about 13% of it.
  • Companion item 9.C — authorizing AHSC agreement execution and application submission for Parcel C East, not to exceed $50,000,000 — approved immediately after as Resolution 2025-29.

Source: City of El Cerrito agenda bills and resolutions, February 7, 2023 and May 6, 2025.

Construction Timeline

Parcel A South

  • September 2024: award announcement states construction “is anticipated to begin in 2025.”
  • Council approvals finalized May 2025.
  • Actual construction start: November 2025 (per BART’s project page).
  • Groundbreaking ceremony: March 5, 2026.
  • Expected completion: Q1 2027 per one City press release; “late 2027” per a separate developer-facing report. Sources do not agree on the completion date — treat as unconfirmed until stated in one place by the City or Related California.

Parcel C East

Roughly a year behind: Round 9 denied December 2025, Round 10 resubmitted 2026, state award decision not expected until December 2026 — meaning construction likely will not start until 2027 at the earliest, assuming it is funded at all.

Funding Outlook — Program-Level Changes in 2026

AHSC is funded entirely through California’s cap-and-invest (formerly cap-and-trade) auction proceeds, not a fixed budget line. Round 10 has about $750 million available, with awards expected December 2026.

Two 2026 developments could affect whether there is a Round 11:

  1. AB 179 splits how AHSC is administered, effective July 1, 2026 — the Strategic Growth Council keeps the “Sustainable Communities” side, while a new Housing Development and Finance Committee takes over the housing-allocation side. How post-award scope changes (like the May 2026 amendment above) get treated under this new split is not yet clear.
  2. CARB’s cap-and-invest program vote, late May 2026, changed the program going forward. Some housing/climate advocates have warned this could substantially reduce or eliminate the auction revenue that funds AHSC, potentially making Round 10 the last well-funded round for the foreseeable future.

Both items should be treated as evolving, not settled — check sgc.ca.gov directly for current status before citing specifics.

Current Funding Picture (FY2026-27/FY2027-28 Biennial Budget)

The City’s Capital Improvement Program budget (Project C3080, “Richmond Street Complete Streets Improvement”) shows the following, current as of the FY2026-27/FY2027-28 biennial budget:

Project description in this budget document: “traffic calming features including chicanes, raised intersections, speed tables, and curb bulb-outs; pedestrian safety features…; enhanced bikeways, specifically bicycle lanes in each direction north of Moeser Lane…approximately 1.85 miles along Elm Street and Richmond Street between the Key Boulevard/Hill Street/Elm Street intersection and the Richmond Street/Fairmount Avenue/Ohlone Greenway intersection.” — the same 1.85-mile corridor as Thread A.

Narrative funding description (Pertinent Issues section): “The City entered into a Cooperative Agreement with the CCTA for CCTA to lead the project given the primary funding source is a Safe Streets for All funding for projects throughout the County.” — this still describes SS4A (Thread A) as the primary funding source.

Funding table (Detailed Breakdown), FY2027 requested:

Source Amount
State Grant (secured) $2,600,600
Capital Improvement Fund (EBMUD) $1,155,600
Measure A Street Improvement Fund $344,730
SB1 – Road Maintenance and Rehabilitation Account $200,000
Miscellaneous Revenue $44,400
Total $4,345,330

The “$2,600,600 State Grant” line is itself broken out elsewhere in the same document as $1,800,600 from AHSC and $800,000 from SB1 LPP, per a March 2026 Council Report — no SS4A line item appears anywhere in this funding table.

This is a genuine, unresolved tension in the source documents, not something reconciled here: the project’s own narrative section names SS4A as the “primary funding source,” but the itemized funding table shows AHSC + SB1 LPP funding instead, with no SS4A line at all. One plausible explanation is that SS4A funds flow through CCTA’s own budget (since CCTA leads that portion under the Cooperative Agreement) and simply don’t appear in the City’s own capital budget table, while the $4.35M shown here represents a separate, City-administered slice of the same overall project. That is a plausible reading, not a confirmed one. The March 2026 Council Report cited in this budget document has not yet been pulled and reviewed directly — that document would resolve this.

Source: FY2026-27/FY2027-28 Biennial Budget, Capital Improvement Program, Project C3080.
